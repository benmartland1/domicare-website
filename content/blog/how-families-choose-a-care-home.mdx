Ask most care home managers how a family finds them and you will get a fair answer: word of mouth, the hospital discharge team, a Google search, the sign outside. All of that still happens. But the order has changed, and the part that decides which homes get a visit now happens somewhere nobody in the building can see.

## The sequence, as it actually runs

Here is what we see repeatedly when we talk to families who have recently placed a parent.

**It starts with a deadline, not a search.** Very few people research care homes casually. The trigger is a fall, a hospital admission, a diagnosis, or a call from a district nurse saying this cannot go on. From that point there is usually a window of days or a couple of weeks, not months.

**The person doing the searching is not the resident.** It is almost always an adult child, typically between 45 and 65, usually working, often not local. They are researching in the evening, on a phone, after a day of other obligations.

**The first move is a question, not a query.** This is the change. Instead of typing "care homes Preston" into Google and working through ten blue links, an increasing number of them type something closer to how they would ask a person: *"best dementia care home near Preston"*, or *"what's the difference between residential and nursing care and which does my mum need"*. They type that into ChatGPT, or into Google's AI Overview, or into Copilot on their work laptop.

**They get an answer, not a list.** The assistant does not return ten options. It returns a paragraph, and inside that paragraph it names two or three homes. That is the shortlist. Everything that follows — the CQC report they read, the reviews they check, the two visits they book — happens inside a set of options that was chosen for them in about four seconds.

**Then they verify.** They look up the named homes on the CQC website. They read reviews on carehome.co.uk. They look at the Google profile. They might, eventually, visit the home's own website — usually to find the phone number and the fee page.

**Then they call.** By the time they reach you, they have already compared you to two other homes, formed a view about your CQC rating, read four reviews, and worked out roughly what they expect to pay.

## Where the enquiries are lost

Most care home marketing is built for the last two steps of that sequence — the website and the phone call. That work is not wasted. But it can only convert a family who reached you, and the losses are happening earlier.

**Loss one: you were not in the answer.** If the assistant named three homes and none of them was yours, no amount of website improvement helps. You were never in the consideration set.

**Loss two: the answer was about you, but wrong or thin.** "There is a care home in the area but I don't have detailed information about it" is a real outcome, and it is worse than not appearing at all — it signals to the family that this one is not worth the click.

**Loss three: your records disagree with each other.** Assistants are conservative. When your CQC record says one thing about your registration, your carehome.co.uk listing says another, and your website implies a third, the safest thing for the model to do is name a competitor it is more confident about.

## Why the records matter more than your website

This is the part that surprises operators most. When an AI assistant answers a question about care in a particular town, it is not primarily reading your website. It is reading:

- Your **CQC provider and location records** — registration, regulated activities, rating, inspection history
- Your **carehome.co.uk listing** and the reviews on it
- Your **Google Business Profile** and Google reviews
- **Local press and community sources**
- And then, if it is readable, your own site

Your website is the source you control most tightly and the one the model trusts least, because it knows you wrote it. The public record is the source you control least and the model trusts most. That is an uncomfortable arrangement, and it is the single most useful thing to understand about care home marketing right now.

The good news is that the public record is not fixed. Registration details, listed specialisms, service descriptions, categories and profile content are all editable, and most homes have never gone through them line by line. When we run a first audit, the most common finding is not that a home is being outcompeted. It is that a home is being *skipped*, because something basic — the specialisms it accepts, the bed count, the fact that it takes nursing as well as residential — is simply not stated anywhere a machine can read it.

## What to do about it this week

You do not need an agency to start on this. Three things are worth doing regardless of who does them.

1. **Ask the assistants yourself.** Open ChatGPT and ask it, in plain language, what a family in your town would ask. "Best care home for dementia in [your town]." "Nursing homes near [your town] with good CQC ratings." Write down which homes it names. If yours is not one of them, you now have the actual problem in front of you rather than a suspicion.

2. **Read your own public record as a stranger.** Pull up your CQC location page, your carehome.co.uk listing and your Google Business Profile side by side. Do they agree on what you are registered for? Do they all mention dementia, or nursing, or respite, if you offer them? Would somebody who had never heard of you be able to tell from those three pages what kind of home this is?

3. **Look at your reviews for substance, not stars.** Assistants quote what reviews *say*. Twenty reviews reading "lovely staff, very happy" give a model nothing to work with. One review that names the dementia unit and describes how a move was handled gives it a sentence it can use. When you ask families for reviews — and you should never incentivise them — ask them what specifically helped, rather than for a rating.

## The honest summary

Nothing about this makes the fundamentals less important. A well-run home with a settled staff team and a good inspection history will always be easier to market than one without. What has changed is that being good is no longer sufficient to be *found*, because the introduction is now made by a system reading structured public data rather than by a person browsing a directory.

Most homes are losing enquiries to a competitor that is not better. It is just legible.
